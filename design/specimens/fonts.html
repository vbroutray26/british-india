Choosing the type for British India

Free alternatives to Ivy Presto with a geometric sans. Every face here is on Google Fonts (SIL Open Font Licence), so it can be self-hosted at no cost. Each pairing also needs Odia and Devanagari partners, because the spec treats the three languages as equals (G-05).

  • Display serifHigh contrast, sharp and elegant, close to Ivy Presto. Used for titles and headings only.
  • SansGeometric, in the Montserrat family style but less common. Used for interface text and data.
  • Passage serifNewsreader stays for long gazetteer quotes, because display faces tire the eye at reading length.
  • Indic partnersAn Odia and a Devanagari face that echo each pairing's mood.

Notes

  • Odia is the hard constraint. No free high-contrast Odia display serif exists. Noto Serif Oriya is the most refined free option, so Odia headings will look calmer than Latin ones in every pairing. A custom Odia display face could be commissioned later.
  • Rozha One is the closest Devanagari match to an Ivy Presto-style face (high contrast, sharp). Tiro Devanagari Hindi is quieter and more bookish.
  • Anek Odia and Anek Devanagari come from the same family, which keeps the sans text in both scripts consistent.
  • Gloock, Libre Caslon Display and Gilda Display ship in one weight with no italic, so the browser fakes the italic subtitle. Instrument Serif and Bodoni Moda have true italics. Per the M0 finding, the final build will not use a faked slant for Odia or Devanagari either way.
  • Size: each Latin display face is about 20–40 KB as a subset WOFF2, within the spec's budget (NFR-PERF-05/07).
